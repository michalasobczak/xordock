# Security analysis — round S008 (2026-11-22)

**Scope:**
- share A copy in iCloud Drive (`BackupPair`, `copySide`, `PruneACopy`, recovery from the A copy);
- CLI and app changes;
- regressions S001–S007.

**Review:**
- code `internal/vault/backup.go`, `recovery.go`, `cmd/xordock`, `macos/`;
- tests;
- E2E with real iCloud Drive.

## Result

No new gaps in the code. The main effect of this round is in the model: **share A now has a copy outside the Mac**, which changes what the loss of a medium gives away. Documentation and procedures were updated (R8-01). No S004–S007 regressions: `go test -race ./...` and `swift test` are green, and the existing backup tests pass unchanged after the refactoring.

| ID | Severity | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| R8-01 | high (model) | The A copy in iCloud + **any** B card (working or spare) makes a complete set. Before S008 a lost card was dangerous only together with the SSD or Time Machine. Now also together with the iCloud account: account takeover or Apple access without ADP. | **documented and mitigated**: a CLI warning on every backup, a one-time confirmation in the app (2FA, ADP, cards kept separately). `docs/recovery.md`: after losing any card, restore the vault with new masks and delete **all** copies of the old A (SSD, iCloud including "Recently Deleted", Time Machine). |
| R8-02 | medium | iCloud keeps deleted files for 30 days, and `backup prune` does not remove them from "Recently Deleted". Old A shares stay in the cloud longer than retention implies (like F-04). | **documented** in `docs/recovery.md`. Removal from "Recently Deleted" requires a manual action, because there is no public API. |
| R8-03 | low | The A copy target is checked by path and `st_dev` (different from the working and spare cards), not by physical disk. | **open → S006**. The target is hard-wired to iCloud Drive on the SSD, so in practice the risk applies only to the CLI with a substituted home directory. |
| R8-04 | low | Recovery from the A copy does not take a lock, so a concurrent `backup prune` could delete objects during reading. | **accepted**: the effect is a read error, never substitute data (`readPair`). |
| R8-05 | informational | The A copy header and point records in iCloud reveal to Apple the vault name and identifier, the counts and times of points (like F-05, R4-08). | **accepted** |

## Checked with no findings

- **No B goes into the A copy (G3):** every copied object passes `decodeShare` for side A, and a test checks that no file of the copy is a B share or contains the marker or the name.
- **The A copy will not open as a live vault** (`Open` rejects the backup role on side B, and `ReadCopyID` rejects a live A).
- **Atomicity in iCloud:** objects are created via tmp + rename, and the point record only after verification. An interruption leaves the previous points recoverable (G5, 3 stages).
- **Retention:** before deleting anything, `PruneACopy` verifies the completeness of the newest point on both sides (like R4-01).
- **App:** still no shell and no reading of shares (S007 G4/G5). ADP warning before the first copy.

## Recommendations for the owner

1. Enable Advanced Data Protection for iCloud and 2FA on the Apple account.
2. Keep `xor_primary` and `xor_backup` in different places. Neither of them may end up in iCloud or on a Time Machine disk.
3. After losing any card: `restore` into a new vault, then delete all copies of the old A.
